# Review 03 — Longer evaluation

Verdict: request revisions before implementation; retain the recommendation to defer integration. The diagnostic replay is useful, but a fair scored experiment needs a more explicit contract. Review used `evidence.md`, the architecture review guide and the current Rust planning code; no build or counterfactual simulation was run.

## Prioritized findings

1. **High — Completing boundary-crossing actions contradicts a fixed common scoring period.** `Prediction::perform` advances and scores an entire action (`goals.rs`), and `WeightedWellbeing` averages over its actual duration. If one candidate finishes work at hour 12.1 while another finishes sleep at hour 16, documenting the rule and reporting durations still compares different periods. A late purchase or completed garment can also receive credit outside the advertised horizon. Required revision: distinguish forecast advancement from the scoring interval. Score exactly `[start, start + 12h]` for every candidate, including a common rule for partial active actions and inventory at that boundary. If that is infeasible without changing prediction semantics, stop at the diagnostic experiment and explicitly defer integration. Test sleep and production that cross the boundary, plus a first goal already longer than twelve hours.

2. **Medium — The continuation is the experimental policy, and “existing rules” does not define it.** Current `search_with_goals` compares prefixes and explores up to two different goal categories (`planning.rs:903`); repeatedly choosing its best result introduces nested search, and forbidding previously used goals would prevent repeated meals or work. A policy that always chooses comfortable sleep can consume the entire forecast and show no payback even when stocked work is feasible. Conversely, forced work can overstate payback by postponing hunger. Required revision: specify the continuation's decision points, needs priorities, legal repeated goals, stable ties, cooldown/prior-action handling, and what happens when no action progresses. Run the same policy from every first-goal endpoint, including sleep, food and production. Do not tune that policy to make Finn produce.

3. **Medium — Bounded branches do not bound continuation cost or make capped results comparable.** Existing production variants still perform procurement, recipe ranking and preparation; repeating them per competitor may multiply the expensive work already identified in the evidence. An action-count cap can leave one candidate at hour four and another at twelve. Required revision: set action/decision limits and define capped forecasts as incomplete diagnostics, excluded from scored comparisons; never silently average their shorter duration or treat their terminal state as twelve hours. Instrument candidate generation and continuation separately, with cold/warm same-snapshot median/p95 timing, action counts and cap hits. Specify stable ordering and repeat identical-snapshot runs to demonstrate reproducibility.

## Simpler route and expected impact

Keep the first step as a standalone fixed-sequence replay, with needs responses and side-by-side sleep/food baselines over the same observation period. Record the actual wellbeing trajectory, spending, wealth, work and residual inputs; find whether and when the procurement deficit is recovered. This establishes whether horizon length is decisive without building a second planner. It does not establish that the current selector will discover or choose that sequence.

For Finn, a longer period could recognize work funded by bulk inputs; the day-14 evidence also shows small positive margins in mixed owned/imported inputs and losses when all eight garment blocks are imported. Longer evaluation cannot repair those losses or guarantee production outranks sleep. The day-16 save must remain a separate observation. Runtime overhead for a manual diagnostic can stay outside normal decisions; integrating continuation adds work roughly with competitors × continuation decisions × existing generation cost, with no measured estimate yet.

## Required revisions

Resolve findings 1–3 in the plan before choosing integration. Carry forward the shared protocol from `evidence.md`, especially both immutable saves, predicted-versus-actual needs and production outcomes, repeated deterministic forecasts, and the same-state performance investigation trigger. Explicitly keep terminal-value changes and production duration changes outside this experiment. Adoption should require fair complete forecasts and measured cost, not a production win. Request fresh review after the contract is specified.

## Fresh review after revision

Verdict: approve the **diagnostic research gate only**. The revised plan resolves the high boundary-comparison finding with an exact common twelve-hour interval and partial-action states. It addresses the medium continuation finding with a declared needs-first, one-goal policy, repeated goals and stable goal-order ties, and the medium cost/completeness finding with explicit caps and whole-comparison fallback. No remaining high or medium issue blocks the diagnostic replay. This verdict does not authorize implementation or approve integration.

Before integration, demonstrate the stated contract rather than treating its bullets as completed work. In particular, verify the normalisation used for needs, deterministic candidate tie ordering within each goal, preservation of cooldowns across continuation decisions, and partial-action accounting at the scoring boundary. Those are implementation acceptance details under the explicit research gate, not reasons to expand the initial diagnostic. Retain both-save validation and cold/warm performance measurements from `evidence.md`; cap fallback must itself be measured because aborted comparisons still incur forecast work.
